{{SEAL}}Lumen

Proposal for Lumen’s second visual pass · 24 October 2026

What the membrane makes, the map spends

The rebuild gave Lumen two good registers on one canvas: Goodsell’s membrane above and Beck’s carbon map below. They don’t yet talk to each other. ATP, NADH and NADPH are what the membrane makes and the map spends, and today they show up only as four bars in a key. This pass would draw that currency as molecules in the stroma between the two registers. It would also bring Goodsell’s style closer, give Lumen the plate furniture Worldline and Miasma already have, and put regulation, stress and the cell’s anatomy into the figure. Every figure below is a live sketch, and most have a Today and a Proposed drawing of the same scene.

Where it stands after a pass through all six specimens

I ran every specimen in light and dark themes, at desktop and phone widths, and set Lumen beside Worldline 2.0 and Miasma. The cross-section is the strongest thing on the page: the phycobilisome fanned under photosystem II, the granum stacked over the thylakoid, and the crista bent by its ATP synthase dimers are drawings no textbook animates. The weak spot is everything between and around the two registers.

What the scene doesn’t say
How the registers connectThe membrane makes ATP and NADPH and the map spends them, but nothing on the canvas moves when the Calvin cycle spends 18 ATP. The only link is the key’s bars.
What’s in the lumenTwo to ten protons in most frames, because ATP synthase drains the gradient as fast as it fills. The complexes’ names sit in the same strip.
Goodsell’s registerEach complex is one lumpy blob with a lighter offset body, so they read as stickers rather than assemblies. The cytoplasm is empty. At 1× the bilayer is an ochre rope, and ochre is also the quinones’ ink and the light’s.
Which pool gates which enzymeA gate shows only when it fully stops an enzyme, as a buffer bar, or as a toast after a click. Partial inhibition, and which pool causes it, never shows.
StressROS and cell health are numbers in the Stats tab. Nothing in the scene changes.
Where the cut isThe sac, granum and crista frames aren’t placed in any cell, so a first-time visitor can’t tell what they are looking at.
Plate furnitureThere is no cartouche and no scale bar, even though the membrane is drawn to scale, and the two registers have no titles. The siblings have all three.

What already works

Two registers under one camera; the six specimens and what each can run; line weight and chevron trains bound to flux; the red spent only on protons; the live caption; the margin’s hairline structure; the warm-up that opens on a living frame. None of it changes, and the proposal builds on all of it.

How the sketches work

The Today drawings port Lumen’s own code (complexes.js, membrane.js, mapdraw.js, the real map layout). The Proposed drawings run on a toy of Lumen’s autoplay: the 0.4 s membrane tick, Calvin’s enzymes in turn, the 1.6 s maintenance drain, the 0.5 s leak and the flux integrator. Their drawing code is written to move into the sim.

The figures are drawn at the sim’s laptop size; scroll each one sideways.

The pools are molecules the currency, drawn between the registers

Lumen’s four cofactor pools are closed and small: 40 ATP and ADP, 40 NAD, 40 NADP, 20 FAD, 140 molecules in all. That is few enough to draw every one. They go in the stroma under the membrane, where the cell keeps them, drawn the way Goodsell draws small molecules. The count in each charged or reduced form always equals the store’s, just as the lumen’s protons equal the gradient.

Then the scene shows its economy directly. ATP synthase pulls in the nearest ADP and lets an ATP go, and FNR reduces an NADP⁺ beside photosystem I. When the Calvin cycle’s PGK spends ATP on the map, a dotted thread runs from the molecule it spent down to the enzyme that spent it. Switch ATP demand off in Fig. 1 and the adenylates fill up, ATP synthase stops, and the lumen floods with red: respiratory control, which today is visible only as a number.

Build

A new src/stroma.js that keeps the molecules and reconciles them with the store each frame, as particles.js already does for protons, with queued events naming a place. The membrane reactions queue theirs through a renderer hook beside spawnProton. The map’s come from onFlux, which already knows each reaction’s segment, so the thread finds its enzyme’s label. The molecules draw after the membrane cache and before the particles; 140 small paths a frame is cheap. They keep clear of the complexes’ stromal parts (the phycobilisome, the F₁ head, complex I’s arm).

What it retires

The key’s pool bars, whose counts move into the cartouche; the floating “NADPH” tag at FNR; the ATP that spills from ATP synthase like seeds; and the map’s yield tags, replaced by its connections (Fig. 4).

Goodsell, closer assemblies, a quieter bilayer, the binding change

Goodsell paints a protein as a set of subunits that read as one molecule. It has a dark outline, flat tones of one colour and lighter seams between the chains, with no gloss. Each complex becomes a part list after its published structure, cut through the middle. Photosystem II is CP43, D1, D2 and CP47 twice, with PsbO and cytochrome c₅₅₀ on the lumenal face. The phycobilisome is stacked phycocyanin discs fanning from an allophycocyanin core. Complex I gets its membrane arm under the transverse helix. The bilayer becomes his pale band, with a row of heads on each face in a warm neutral, so ochre is left to the quinones and the light. Individual tails appear only once you zoom in.

ATP synthase’s head shows Boyer’s binding change. Its α₃β₃ ring is a stator, and each third of a turn of γ steps every β subunit from open to loose to tight. The front two show it: ADP and phosphate in the loose one, ATP in the tight one, and the ATP let go as that β swings open. Past 2.5× zoom the sim would draw what is inside each complex, with the subunits named and the cofactors in place. The electron then steps site to site, where today it slides between complexes. Press Zoomed in for a tracking shot along its road.

Build

complexes.js trades its blobs for part lists and one assembly() painter; membrane.js keeps its layout and cache. The binding change is a live layer over the head, next to the rotor. The zoom layer is a pass in drawLive that runs when camera.zoom ≥ 2.5. Its cofactor positions are approximate, after the structures. particles.js interpolates an electron’s position through the sites, so the step from P680 to Qʙ happens inside PSII’s footprint.

Not in the sketch

The mitochondrial complexes (III’s matrix core proteins, IV’s Cuᴀ domain, a proper complex II) and the purple membrane’s lattice get the same treatment in the build. So does the S-layer, which today reads as a row of tables and should be a continuous canopy with pores.

The plate around it callouts, a cartouche, a scale, a locator

Worldline 2.0 and Miasma are plates: a reference cartouche in a double hairline, a scale bar, numbered figures. Lumen has the numbered figure and nothing else. Fig. 1 shows the rest. The complexes’ names move out of the lumen into a row of callouts above the cut, with hairline leaders down to each complex, so the lumen holds only protons. The registers get titles, a for the cross-section and b for the network, with a hairline between them. Panel a has a 10 nm scale bar, since it is drawn to scale; the map isn’t, so it has none. The key becomes a cartouche like its siblings’, its rows only for what the scene shows, and it carries the pools’ counts.

A locator answers where the cut comes from. In the map’s empty upper right, a line drawing of the cell or organelle has the section boxed, the way a map carries an inset of its region. The same drawings can head the margin’s specimen list, as Miasma’s topology glyphs head its margin.

Connections and gates the map says what it spends and what stops it

Beck’s map marks where a line meets another service. Lumen’s map can do the same for the pools. Each enzyme that makes or spends a cofactor carries that molecule’s glyph after its name, signed: PGK +2 ATP, GAPDH +2 NADH. Running backward the signs and cofactors change, so GAPDH spends NADPH for the Calvin cycle. The glyph pulses when the enzyme fires, which retires the floating yield tags and their collisions. These are the same glyphs as the stroma’s molecules, so the eye links the registers by shape.

Regulation becomes visible on the track. Whenever regulation.js returns less than 1, the effector’s glyph sits across the line from the enzyme’s name, with ⊣ when it inhibits (PFK under ATP, IDH under NADH, G6PDH under NADPH), → when it is required (RuBisCO’s activase needs ATP), and ⊕ when it spurs (G6PDH at low NADPH). The mark is dashed while the enzyme is only slowed and solid, with today’s buffer bar, once it stops. Move the sliders under Fig. 4 to watch PFK go from free to slowed to stopped as ATP rises.

Where the specimen has compartments, the map shows them as fare zones. In the animal cell the mitochondrial matrix is a zone whose boundary is drawn as a bilayer in miniature, and pyruvate crosses it to reach PDH. The prokaryotes get no zones, which is itself the point.

Collisions fixed

FBPase off F6P’s capsule; TK + SBPase off the corridor (p10 to grid 6.35, 1.62); HACD off Keto-CoA; PRK lifted from the Calvin track; the phone overrides for 6PGL, G6PDH and the Calvin loop’s name. Without yield tags there is nothing left to cross a track.

A bug it surfaces

PK’s step-9 gate applies in both directions (AGENTS.md, open work), so today’s map already draws a buffer bar across PK whenever ATP falls below 30%, and a click on PK is refused then, though only PC + PEPCK needs the ATP. Gate marks would make it plainer still. The build fixes the gate to apply in reverse only.

The margin’s figure electrons by redox potential

Miasma’s margin opens on a figure of its own, Nightingale’s rose, while Lumen’s Stats tab is all bars and numbers. The Z-scheme is the figure that belongs there: every carrier at its midpoint potential, with each electron now in flight in the membrane drawn as a bead on the same ladder. Light lifts the bead at P680 and again at P700, cyclic flow loops it back from ferredoxin to plastoquinone, and respiration (in indigo) only ever falls, from NADH to oxygen. A student can then see why the photosystems need light and why oxygen is the sink. The mitochondrion gets complexes I to IV, ubiquinone and cytochrome c on the same axis. The purple membrane shows an empty ladder with a note, since bacteriorhodopsin pumps without electrons.

Build

A canvas at the top of the Stats tab (src/dashboard.js), drawn only while the tab is open, reading the same electrons particles.js draws. Each chain maps its membrane segments to runs of ladder nodes, and the sketch carries the mapping for the linear, respiratory and cyclic chains. The potentials are textbook midpoints at pH 7. The reference overlay should cite where they come from before this ships; I haven’t taken a citation from memory.

What moves

The pools’ sparklines stay below it as a small strip chart of their own. The counters and the ATP-source split stay. The last-reaction block moves to the bottom, since the map’s pulsing glyphs now say what fired.

Stress, drawn radicals in the matrix, damage in the bilayer

ROS gets a body: each unit of active ROS (produced minus scavenged) becomes a superoxide radical, two atoms and the radical’s dot. It appears beside complex I or the Q-cycle, where Lumen’s leaks happen, and drifts until it is scavenged. Cell health becomes lipid peroxidation, the damage ROS actually does. The share of tails drawn kinked at a peroxide, in a darker sienna, is the share of health lost, and they heal as health regenerates. This is a pattern before it is a hue, and it reads in grayscale. Uncoupling and oxidative stress, now among the least visible switches, become two of the most visible.

Order of work

  1. The plate. Callouts, panel titles, the scale bar and the cartouche; the label collisions; idle line weight. Small, safe, and the most consistency per hour.
  2. The stroma’s pools. src/stroma.js, the docking hooks, the threads; the map’s connection glyphs; the key’s bars and the yield tags retired.
  3. Goodsell, closer. Part lists for every complex in all six specimens, the bilayer, the binding change, the S-layer.
  4. Gates and zones on the map, with the PK gate fixed.
  5. Locators on the plate and, if wanted, as the margin’s specimen icons.
  6. The margin’s Z-scheme, potentials cited.
  7. Stress: radicals and peroxidised lipids.
  8. The zoom layer: cofactors, subunit names, electron waypoints.
  9. Phones, docs, verify. All six specimens in both themes, phone 390, reduced motion, 60 fps on the default preset, no console errors; AGENTS.md and about.md; commit inside projects/lumen and stage only its pointer in the parent.

For you to decide

The threads

The dotted threads from a molecule to the enzyme that changed it make the coupling literal, but at high flux they add motion between the registers. Keep them, or let the shared glyphs carry the link alone?

Callouts or lumen labels

Callouts above the cut free the lumen for the red, at the cost of leaders crossing it. I prefer the callouts. Today’s in-lumen labels are the alternative.

The zoom layer

Cofactors, subunit names and the stepping electron are the most work for a view only zoomers see. It’s last in the order. Build it, or stop after step 7?

How crowded

140 molecules is honest to the model but sparse next to Goodsell’s cytoplasm. Larger pools (say 2× in the model) would crowd the stroma and smooth the ratios, but would change the regulation thresholds’ feel.

The c-ring

Per-specimen rings and H⁺/ATP: a real science improvement, but a model change. In or out of this pass?

Specimen icons

Use the locator drawings as the margin’s specimen list, as Miasma does with its topologies, or keep the text list?
