# Lumen

Interactive cellular-metabolism simulator at a9l.im/lumen. One Canvas 2D plate in two registers split at the membrane: above (panel a), the specimen's membrane in cross-section in the manner of David Goodsell, with the cell's cofactor pools as molecules under it; below (panel b), the cell's carbon network as a transit map. What the membrane makes, the map spends. Under the drawing runs a stiff kinetic model of the cell (`src/engine/`), on a clock from 300× slower than life to 1,000× faster; the visitor pulses, inhibits or holds enzymes and changes light, oxygen and demand, and every moving thing reads a quantity in the model. Vanilla ES modules, no dependencies; KaTeX from jsDelivr renders the reference overlay.

It is a submodule of the a9l.im repository (`projects/lumen`). The root `AGENTS.md` covers the shared layer, the design system ("Inside the scene"), build and deployment; the parent's `docs/sim-overhaul/PLAN.md` is the ratified plan the rebuild followed. `docs/VISION.md` is a9's roadmap and decisions.

## Running and checking

Preview through the parent build (serve `dist/`, open `/lumen/`), or from the working tree with `node docs/sim-overhaul/serve.mjs 8791` in the parent; the parent's `docs/sim-overhaul/check.mjs` takes screenshots and reports console errors, overflow and fps. There is no linter.

```bash
node --test tests/                     # headless, Node 22+, no install; about 13 s
for f in main.js colors.js src/*.js src/engine/*.js; do node --check "$f"; done
git diff --check
```

`tests/` holds only checks whose failure wouldn't show on the plate: the network's structure (`network`: element and charge balance against BiGG's formulas, the moieties, no conservation law nobody specified; `thermo`: no cycle with a ΔG'° of its own), the integrator's guarantees, and the archetypes' classic behaviours (`behavior`; VISION.md's table). Add a test where a failure would be silent, one per mechanism, and never a snapshot of a tuned value.

## The model

The engine is authoritative and headless: nothing under `src/engine/` touches the page, nothing outside it writes engine state except through cell.js, and nothing reads `store` back into the model.

- **Species and compartments.** BiGG ids (`atp`, `g6p`, `13dpg`), with formulas and charges from BiGG's iJO1366 (iJN678 for RuBP) so balance is checked against an independent source; the protein carriers (plastocyanin, standing for cytochrome c and halocyanin too, and ferredoxin) are written by hand. Compartments: `c` cytosol, `m` the matrix (animal cell only), `l` the lumen side of the drawn membrane, `e` the medium; gases and small permeant molecules live in `c`. Water and buffered H⁺ are fixed; `h_l`, the protons translocated across the drawn membrane, is a species. Glycogen (standing for starch too) is a `polymer` counted in glucosyl units: it enters saturation but cancels from the mass-action ratio, since adding or taking a unit leaves the chains' ends as they were, so the store's size doesn't move phosphorylase's or synthase's equilibrium.
- **Reactions.** One enzyme's reaction each, so opposing enzymes (hexokinase and G6Pase, PFK and FBPase, the two GAPDHs) are separate rows. A tag places a species in another compartment (`pyr[c] -> pyr[m]`). FAD stays bound; GTP counts as ATP; membrane reactions are written per two electrons. The biomass row (E. coli core composition) and the demand rows are sinks, exempt from balance: biomass takes its nitrogen as glutamate and glutamine (returning the α-KG their transaminations free) and its lipid as palmitoyl-CoA, so its lumped costs leave out what GS–GOGAT and fatty-acid synthesis spend.
- **Thermodynamics.** Metabolite ΔG'° from eQuilibrator (equilibrator-api 0.8.1, pH 7, I 0.25 M, pMg 3); the membrane's from midpoint potentials against NAD, with photons' energies for the photosystems. Consistent around every cycle.
- **Rates** are separable, v = Vmax · activity · η_reg · η_sat · η_thermo: convenience-kinetics saturation, and η_thermo = 1 − e^(ΔG/RT) in the Haldane form, finite at zero concentration, zero at equilibrium and never against ΔG. ΔG includes Δp: each charge moved to the lumen side costs F·Δψ and each proton F·ΔpH. Regulation is smooth effectors (inhibitors, activators, ratios, light, Δp). A `brake` (AtpΘ, CF₁) reads the reaction's own e^(ΔG/RT) and multiplies both directions, so it stops a stalled synthase splitting ATP without ever letting a flux run against ΔG. A demand row runs at its dial's rate, paced by its scarcest precursor (Liebig). Light has two switches: thioredoxin's (cooperative, half set at 5% sun) for the enzymes light turns on, and a lower, steeper one (half set at 1%, I⁴) for those it turns off, so at dusk the Calvin cycle closes before the night's pathways open; with one switch both ran half on and cycled CO₂ between them. The carboxylases take bicarbonate, which isn't carried, so they bind CO₂ at 2 µM in its place. The cyanobacterium's pyruvate kinase keyed on RuBP is phenomenological (it stands for PirC and PII; VISION.md has why).
- **The flagship's electrons.** Ferredoxin's partners (NDH-1, PGR5's cyclic flow, the flavodiiron outlet) bind its oxidized form as weakly as FNR does, so a mostly oxidized pool doesn't hold them back and FNR doesn't take every electron: in dim light cyclic flow and NDH-1 make the ATP the Calvin cycle needs beyond linear flow's. At night NDH-1 takes NADPH's electrons through FNR run backward. The oxidase is small beside PSI, so dim light's electrons don't go to it, and lactate dehydrogenase binds pyruvate weakly, so the cell ferments only once PDH stops for want of NAD⁺. β-oxidation's electrons reach the quinone pool through ETF (a conserved matrix pool) and ETF-QO.
- **Allocation.** In the cyanobacterium growth takes the day's carbon first and the store only the surplus: growth runs in the light and slows as ATP/ADP falls, ADP-glucose pyrophosphorylase needs 3-PGA, phosphorylase opens as AMP rises, citrate synthase gives way to α-KG so α-KG goes to nitrogen, and GS reads α-KG over glutamine as PII does. In the animal cell the cytosol's acetyl-CoA comes from citrate (the electroneutral citrate carrier, ATP-citrate lyase, the cytosol's MDH); long-chain acyl-CoA holds ACC at growth's µM, and CPT binds palmitoyl-CoA at tens of µM, so it takes fat from the medium fast and the cell's own slowly.
- **Δp = Δψ + ΔpH** (mV), each its own signed state: Δψ is κψ times `dq_l`, the net charge moved to the lumen side (each reaction's `z`, carriers between cytosol and matrix included); ΔpH is κH times `h_l` (`nH`). Counter-ion channels (`ION`, on Δψ) and K⁺/H⁺ exchange (`KHE`, on ΔpH) set how Δp divides, about Δψ/ΔpH = g(KHE)/g(ION): mostly Δψ in mitochondria and bacteria, mostly ΔpH in thylakoids. K⁺ is held on both sides.
- **The c-ring.** Each cell's `ring` sets ATP synthase's protons per ATP, ring/3 (model.js rewrites its equation); Fig. 2 derives P/O from the cell's own coupling (`engine.coupling(j)`).
- **Integration.** ROS2 (linearly implicit Rosenbrock, L-stable) with a finite-difference Jacobian assembled reaction by reaction (stored and factored dense). Rosenbrock steps keep every linear conservation law exactly; a step that would make a species negative is retried smaller. Steps are error-controlled (RTOL 2e-3) up to `HMAX`, 60 s, and `H_PULSE`, 0.05 s, while a pulse fades (a step holds a pulse's factor where it started). Two clocks: the integrator's, and the picture's (`engine.t`, `state.x`), which is the straight line between the last two steps, so it too keeps the conservation laws and every sign. A step's size depends only on the state, so a trajectory is the same however frames slice time. `advance(dt, ms)` stops stepping after `ms` of wall time; the picture waits where the integrator got to, the rest is dropped (not owed), and it returns the shortfall. Anything changed from outside (state, environment, a dial, a switch, damage, a pulse) happens at the picture's time: `advance` pins the integrator back to it with short steps. main.js gives it 8 ms a frame (`ENGINE_MS`). `createEngine(id, { hmax, rtol })` gives a tighter integrator to check against.
- **The verb.** `pulse(j, ±1)` multiplies activity by 5 at its peak (or 0.1), fading over 1.5 model seconds, stacking; `pulse(j, sign, true)` sustains ×(1 ± `HOLD`, 0.1) until `release(j)`. Pulses are functions of model time, so they freeze with the clock and keep trajectories frame-independent. `snapshot()`/`restore()` copy everything a trajectory depends on except held pulses, for the paired baseline (not save and replay).
- **The hold** (compare.js) runs a held and an unheld copy in a worker until every flux in both is still (under 0.1% a second for 5 s, up to 900 model s), then reports flux control coefficients, C = ln(J_held/J_base)/ln 1.1 (Kacser & Burns 1973). The copies step at most 2 s: their fluxes are read on the picture, where a fast enzyme near equilibrium saws with a long step and is never still. Stillness isn't convergence: the animal cell's PFK over glucose uptake moves another 11% by 1,800 s.
- **Damage.** Superoxide from complex I and the Q-cycle climbs steeply with Δp; what SOD, catalase and glutathione peroxidase leave peroxidises the membrane, raising the leak and inactivating aconitase. Repair is a demand row that spends ATP, so a cell without ATP stays damaged.
- **Randomness** (rng.js, seeded) feeds only the window's sampling, never the chemistry.

## The window

window.js writes `store`, the picture panel a draws; display noise never reaches the model.

- Counts are true: each pool's drawn total is its mean count in a slab the cut's length by 16.5 nm by 20 nm deep, in the compartment under the membrane, its forms in the cell's ratio. The exception is the lumen's protons, one per 2 mV of Δp (`MV_PER`), since almost all translocated protons are bound to buffers.
- Each drawn complex is a representative centre turning over at `KCAT` times the share of its base capacity in use, times the clock's speed (`Win.speed`). Its own sampled turnovers drive what it does in the scene; an effect is never borrowed from another complex's rate. Past `MAX_RATE` (8 a second) one turnover in w is drawn and stands for w, and the caption says so.
- An electron waits at each later complex until that complex turns over. A turnover no drawn electron meets within 0.6 s takes one from its donor's pool (a short hop); an electron that waits 3 s fades into its carrier's pool.
- Map reactions that change a drawn pool are sampled at their one-way rates (at most `MAX_EVENTS`, 3, a second per reaction, pool and direction), so an enzyme at equilibrium still turns molecules over. Forward and backward events pair up: `d` ±1 changes a molecule, ±0.5 is an exchange (a thread only). `Renderer.mapEvent` passes `d` through; rounding it to a sign brings back the strobe of molecules flipping back and forth.
- A docking complex takes the nearest molecule and lets it go changed as far from the site as it came from, so a busy complex doesn't gather the pool around it (the real pools mix in microseconds, far faster than the drawn drift).
- Motion is continuous: nothing but a genuinely new object moves more than about 3 nm a frame, and nothing appears at full opacity. Carriers are persistent instances that travel, dock, ride and wander back; protons the pumps owe cross up through the complexes pumping.

## Conventions

- A new reaction goes in network.js balanced in elements and charge (add a new species' BiGG id to tools/bigg.mjs and regenerate formulas.js), into the cells that have it (cells.js), with its ΔG'° (thermo.js) and kinetics (rates.js). Run the tests: a new conservation law nobody specified, or a cycle with a ΔG'° of its own, is a finding.
- Parameters are illustrative unless a comment cites a source; a measured value names it.
- Every effect in the scene reads a quantity in the model (root AGENTS.md, "Inside the scene"); a new one names its quantity in a comment where it is drawn. The accent is Lumen's one red and means protons; canvas code reads the palette through `readPalette()` on each theme change.
- The legend and caption follow "Show, don't tell": the caption is Fig. 1's live values in a line (Δp, ATP synthase's drawn turns a second, thinning, the clock's real speed while the chemistry lags) and doesn't repeat what panel a's title, the legend or the key shows.
- colors.js is a deferred classic script ordered before the `main.js` module; its `_freezeTokens()` call freezes `_PALETTE`, so new palette keys go above it.
- The about-panel `lastUpdated` is in `src/ui.js`; the parent build rewrites it, with index.html's title, descriptions and `dateModified`, from about.md's frontmatter.
- A new membrane complex touches specimens.js (`WIDTH`, the specimens' `order` and `names`), complexes.js (its part list and painter), membrane.js (`drawStatic`, its anchor height in `layout`, its hit box, `TOP_NM` for its callout, its stromal parts in `_stroma`, a docking site if it changes a pool), detail.js (cofactor sites and subunits), ladder.js (its carriers), renderer.js (`_ETC_INFO`), info.js (`ENZYMES`), mapbind.js (`COMPLEX`), rates.js (`KCAT`), window.js if its turnovers draw something new, and the network for its chemistry.
- A new map segment touches maplayout.js (its track's `segs`, a station if new), mapbind.js (`segments`, `STATION` if new), map.js (`LABEL_AT`, phone overrides, `FLIP`) and renderer.js (`_SEG_INFO`).

## Known approximations

To revisit as the engine matures; VISION.md has the open questions they raise.

- Most kinetic parameters (rates.js) and pool sizes (cells.js) are illustrative; SABIO-RK's constants and measured pools should replace them. The biomass row is E. coli's core composition in every cell.
- The cyanobacterium has no carbon-concentrating mechanism, so at full sun its CO₂ is drawn down to about 3 µM (milestone 3).
- Over a day the cyanobacterium's growth takes about 63% of the carbon it fixes and its store 27%; the store still gains about 850 mM of glucosyl units a day and nothing dilutes it, since growth doesn't enlarge the cell or account its products (milestone 4).
- Pyruvate kinase keyed on RuBP starves growth below about a fifth of full sun, where dim light is bistable between growing and storing. FBPase's AMP brake is the liver enzyme's, standing in for an energy homeostat. Between 40% and 42% sun the energy charge cycles (0.76–0.88, about 106 s; a tight integrator agrees), and from 40% to 44% pyruvate climbs to 16–20 mM behind a PDH held by acetyl-CoA. A step from full sun straight to 2–5% takes the energy charge to about 0.6 before it recovers; the sun's own dusk doesn't fall below 0.84.
- K⁺ is held on both sides, so the counter-ion conductances are standing leaks. The night's budget (maintenance demand, the mid-morning store, the thylakoid's leak) is illustrative and sets how long a night lasts; PFK's and the oxidase's capacities, LDH's pyruvate Km, ferredoxin's partners' binding (FNR's value, lent) and the brake's −3 kJ/mol are calibrated toward the night's targets, each answering a mechanism found by ablation. The chloroplast exports its starch as triose, standing for maltose and glucose, faster than a clock-paced leaf. IF1 is a Δp gate, where AtpΘ and CF₁ are brakes. The composite cell in anoxic dark runs out of ATP.
- Identities simplify by design (decision 14): the cyanobacterium keeps the textbook Krebs ring and L-lactate.
- Fatty-acid synthesis runs on acyl-CoA, not ACP, lumped per round. With no lipid store, the animal cell burns about 90% of the fatty acid it makes. Without fat in its medium it grows at about a quarter of its dial until its glucose runs out (about 25 minutes), then runs down until fed. The composite cell's synthesis and β-oxidation share a compartment and cycle at C4, so it doesn't grow. The matrix's carriers are lumped, and the citrate route isn't drawn.
- The halophile has no amino-acid metabolism, so without light it has nothing to run on.
- PSII's centre states aren't modeled; the hold's steady state checks fluxes only; Fig. 2's O₂ is PSII's gross production, not net exchange.
- The pulse's size and fade and the hold's 10% are first guesses, to tune by eye.
