# Lumen

Interactive cellular-metabolism simulator at a9l.im/lumen. The scene is one Canvas 2D plate in two registers split at the membrane: above (panel a), the specimen's membrane in cross-section, drawn in the manner of David Goodsell (lipids, complexes and carriers at roughly true relative size), with the cell's cofactor pools as molecules in the space under it; below (panel b), the cell's whole carbon network as a transit map. What the membrane makes, the map spends: the same molecules link the two. Under the drawing runs a kinetic model of the cell's chemistry (src/engine/): every reaction at a thermodynamically consistent rate, on its own clock, from 300× slower than life to 1,000× faster. The user pulses or inhibits enzymes, changes the light, the oxygen and the cell's demands, and every moving thing in the figure reads a quantity in the model. Vanilla ES modules with no dependencies of its own; KaTeX from jsDelivr renders the reference overlay.
It is a submodule of the a9l.im repository (`a9lim.github.io`, at `projects/lumen`); the root `AGENTS.md` covers the shared layer, design system ("Inside the scene"), build, and deployment, and `docs/sim-overhaul/PLAN.md` the ratified plan this rebuild follows.

## Running and checking

Preview through the parent build (serve `dist/`, open `/lumen/`), or from the working tree with `node docs/sim-overhaul/serve.mjs 8791` in the parent and `docs/sim-overhaul/check.mjs` for screenshots, console errors, overflow and fps. The engine's tests run headless in node (no dependencies; `tests/` isn't staged); there is no linter. Check with:

```bash
node --test tests/*.test.mjs
for f in main.js colors.js src/*.js src/engine/*.js; do node --check "$f"; done
git diff --check
```

## How it works

### The engine

src/engine/ is a kinetic model of the cell, headless and authoritative (VISION.md, milestone 1). Its parts:

- **Species and compartments.** species.js names every chemical (BiGG ids: `atp`, `g6p`, `13dpg`, …) with its formula and charge from formulas.js, generated from BiGG's iJO1366 (iJN678 for RuBP) by tools/bigg.mjs; the protein carriers (plastocyanin, standing for cytochrome c and halocyanin too, and ferredoxin) are written by hand, their forms differing by one electron. `MOIETIES` are the conserved groups (adenine, NAD, NADP, CoA, quinone, plastocyanin, ferredoxin), specified by hand so the tests can check the network keeps them. model.js places each species in a compartment: `c` the cytosol, `m` the mitochondrial matrix (the animal cell only), `l` the lumen side of the drawn membrane, `e` the medium. Gases and small permeant molecules live in `c`. Water and buffered H⁺ are fixed; `h_l`, the protons translocated across the drawn membrane, is a species. Glycogen (standing for starch too) is a `polymer`, counted in glucosyl units: its amount enters a reaction's saturation where it is a substrate but cancels from the mass-action ratio, since adding or taking a unit leaves the chains' ends as they were, so phosphorylase's and synthase's equilibria don't depend on how much is stored.
- **Reactions.** network.js lists every reaction as an equation over species, one enzyme's reaction each (VISION.md: identities first): hexokinase and G6Pase, PFK and FBPase, PK and PC + PEPCK, the NAD- and NADP-linked GAPDHs, β-oxidation (resolved by chain length, C16 to C4) and fatty-acid synthesis (lumped per round, on its own enzymes) are separate rows. A tag places a species in another compartment (`pyr[c] -> pyr[m]`). FAD stays bound: SDH and the acyl-CoA dehydrogenase hand their electrons straight to the quinone pool. GTP counts as ATP. The membrane's reactions are written per two electrons. The biomass row (E. coli core composition, nitrogen folded onto α-KG, NADPH and ATP) and the demand rows are sinks.
- **Cells.** cells.js gives each preset's organism its reactions (from sets: glycolysis, the two halves of the PPP, the Calvin cycle, Krebs, β-oxidation, fatty-acid synthesis, the chains, ROS, the matrix's carriers), compartments and volumes, its carriers' midpoint potentials, its conserved pools (mM, tagged measured or illustrative), environment, medium, κ, ATP demand and growth defaults, and kinetic overrides (thioredoxin's light switch on plastid and cyanobacterial enzymes, IF1 on the mitochondrial ATP synthase, AtpΘ and CF₁'s brakes on the cyanobacterium's and the chloroplast's, RuBisCO activase in the chloroplast). The photosynthetic cells start with a mid-morning store of glycogen or starch (300 mM of glucosyl units), enough for a night.
- **Thermodynamics.** thermo.js has every metabolite reaction's ΔG'° from eQuilibrator (equilibrator-api 0.8.1, pH 7, I 0.25 M, pMg 3, computed by Codex) and the membrane's from midpoint potentials against NAD (with photons' energies for the photosystems, and bacteriorhodopsin stalling near 280 mV), consistent around every cycle.
- **Rates.** Each rate is separable: v = Vmax · activity · η_reg · η_sat · η_thermo, with convenience-kinetics saturation and η_thermo = 1 − e^(ΔG/RT), computed in the Haldane form so it is finite at zero concentration, zero at equilibrium and never against ΔG. ΔG includes the proton-motive force: each charge the reaction moves into the lumen costs F·Δp. Regulation is smooth effectors (rates.js: inhibitors, activators, ratios such as NADH/NAD⁺, light, Δp). Thioredoxin's light switch is cooperative, L = I²/(I² + 0.05²), so by a fifth of full sun the enzymes light turns off (G6PDH, PFK, phosphorylase) are off, and in the dark CP12 holds the Calvin cycle's PRK and NADP-GAPDH to a thousandth. A `brake` (AtpΘ, CF₁) holds a reaction against running backward: it reads the reaction's own e^(ΔG/RT), the backward rate over the forward, is half bound at ΔG = −3 kJ/mol and multiplies both directions, so it stops a stalled ATP synthase splitting ATP without ever letting a flux run against ΔG. The store is regulated as a store: ADP-glucose pyrophosphorylase needs 3-PGA, so synthesis takes what the Calvin cycle makes beyond its needs, and phosphorylase opens as AMP rises. Gases diffuse; the leaks are conductances; demand rows (ATPM, biomass, the plastid's triose-phosphate export, repair) run at their dials' rates, the biomass row at the pace of its scarcest precursor.
- **Δp = Δψ + ΔpH** (in mV), each from its own state. Δψ is κψ times `dq_l`, the net charge moved to the lumen side: each reaction moves z, the charge on the lumen species it makes or spends (translocated protons, the lumen's carriers, counter-ions) plus any its carriers move between cytosol and matrix (`z` in network.js), so complex I moves four, the Q-cycle's complex III two and complex IV four. ΔpH is κH times `h_l`, the protons the lumen side has gained, mostly bound to buffers. Both states are signed. Moving a proton costs F·Δp, a charge alone F·Δψ (ANT's ATP⁴⁻ for ADP³⁻), and a proton with a counter-charge F·ΔpH (the pyruvate and phosphate carriers). Counter-ion channels (`ION`, on Δψ) and K⁺/H⁺ exchange (`KHE`, on ΔpH) set how Δp divides, about Δψ/ΔpH = g(KHE)/g(ION) where nothing else spends one part alone (cells.js): mostly Δψ in mitochondria and bacteria (about 160 and 0.5 pH units in the animal cell at steady state), mostly ΔpH in thylakoids (about 2.3 units). Raising KHE is nigericin; raising ION is valinomycin with K⁺. K⁺ (standing for every counter-ion) is held on both sides.
- **Entries to the quinone pool.** Acyl-CoA dehydrogenase hands its electrons to ETF (`etfox`/`etfred`, a conserved pool in the matrix, −25 mV), and ETF:quinone oxidoreductase (`ETFQO`, not drawn) passes them on, so β-oxidation backs up when the quinone pool is reduced. The cyanobacterium's NDH-1 takes ferredoxin (`NDH1F`; Schuller et al. 2019): in the light from PSI, as cyclic flow that pumps, and in the dark from NADPH through FNR run backward (FNR's weak binding of oxidized ferredoxin gives that direction its capacity). NDH-2 (`NDH2`, NADH to the quinone pool, no pumping) is a minor route there. Ferredoxin's other partners (NDH-1, PGR5's cyclic flow, the flavodiiron outlet) bind its oxidized form as weakly as FNR does, so a mostly oxidized pool doesn't hold them back and FNR doesn't take every electron: in dim light cyclic flow and NDH-1 make the ATP the Calvin cycle needs beyond linear flow's. At night the cyanobacterium respires its glycogen through the oxidative pentose phosphate pathway into NDH-1, at about 5% of its daytime O₂ evolution, on a maintenance demand (0.05 mM/s); its ATP synthase makes ATP on respiration, and AtpΘ, a `brake`, stops it running backward, so in anoxia it ferments, keeps its ATP and lets Δp go. Its oxidase's capacity is a night's respiration's few times over, small beside PSI's, so in dim light it doesn't take light's electrons; its lactate dehydrogenase binds pyruvate weakly and runs only once PDH stops for want of NAD⁺. The window draws an NDH-1 or PGR5 electron from ferredoxin, on to the oxidase or back to PSI as those two take plastocyanin's electrons (in the dark, all to the oxidase).
- **The c-ring.** Each cell's `ring` sets ATP synthase's protons per ATP, ring/3 (model.js rewrites its equation): c₈ in the animal cell, c₁₄ in the chloroplast and *Synechocystis*, c₁₀ (*E. coli*'s) in the anaerobe, an illustrative c₁₂ in the halophile, whose A-type ring is uncounted; sources in cells.js. Fig. 2's ladder view derives the P/O ratio (or ATP per NADPH) from the cell's own coupling (`engine.coupling(j)`): 30/11 from NADH in the animal cell, counting the phosphate carrier and ANT as one more proton per ATP exported.
- **Integration.** engine.js integrates by ROS2 (linearly implicit Rosenbrock, L-stable) with a sparse finite-difference Jacobian; the system is stiff, and Rosenbrock steps keep every linear conservation law exactly. Steps are error-controlled (RTOL 2e-3) up to `HMAX`, 60 s, and `H_PULSE` (0.05 s) while a pulse fades, since a step holds a pulse's factor where it started; steps that would make a species negative are retried smaller. Two clocks: the integrator's and the picture's (`engine.t`, `state.x`). `advance(dt, ms)` steps the integrator past the picture's new time, and a step's size depends only on the state, so its trajectory is the same however frames slice time; the picture is the straight line between its last two steps (dense output, which keeps the conservation laws and every concentration's sign). Given `ms`, it stops stepping after that much wall time, the picture waits where the integrator got to and the rest is dropped; it returns the model seconds it fell short by. Anything changed from outside (the state, the environment, a dial, a switch, the damage, a pulse) happens at the picture's time: `advance` notices it and `pin()`s the integrator back to the picture with short steps (`H0`). Damage is solved exactly over a step. `createEngine(id, { hmax, rtol })` gives a tighter integrator to check against (tests).
- **The verb.** `pulse(j, sign)` multiplies a reaction's activity by 1 + 4 at its peak (or 1 − 0.9 to inhibit), fading with a 1.5 s model-time constant; pulses stack. `pulse(j, sign, true)` instead sustains the small perturbation of metabolic control analysis, ×(1 ± `HOLD`, 0.1), until `release(j)`. Being functions of model time, pulses keep trajectories frame-independent and freeze with the clock. `snapshot()` and `restore()` copy everything a trajectory depends on (state, clock, environment, dials, fading pulses), for the paired baseline. Switches (a line knocked out, UCP) set `state.activity`.
- **Damage.** Superoxide from complex I and the Q-cycle (both climbing steeply with Δp) is taken by SOD, catalase and glutathione peroxidase on NADPH; what remains peroxidises the membrane (`state.damage`), which raises the leak and inactivates aconitase. Repair is a demand row that spends ATP, and damage clears only as fast as that row runs, so a cell without ATP stays damaged.
- **Randomness.** Seeded streams (rng.js) feed only the window's sampling, never the chemistry.

### The window

window.js is panel a as a window onto the cell (VISION.md): it writes `store`, the picture, which nothing in the engine reads. The slab is the cut's length (the specimen's complexes and gaps, fixed) by the band's 16.5 nm by 20 nm deep. Each pool's drawn total is its mean count in the slab at the cell's concentrations, in the compartment under the membrane (the matrix in the animal cell), and its forms follow the cell's ratio; FAD stays bound, so no FAD is drawn. Stations show their species' mean counts in the slab (the storage polymer's glucosyl units where a cell keeps glycogen or starch). The lumen's protons are one per 2 mV of Δp (`MV_PER`), not a count: almost all translocated protons are bound to buffers.

Each drawn complex is a representative centre: it turns over at its turnover number (`KCAT`, rates.js) times the share of its base capacity in use, times the clock's speed (`Win.speed`, model seconds a wall second; 30× slower by default, the conditions'). Above eight a second (`MAX_RATE`) one turnover in w is drawn and stands for w (`Win.thin`; the rotor turns w thirds), and the caption says so. Each complex's own sampled turnovers drive what it does in the scene, so a drawn effect is never borrowed from another complex's rate. A chain's entry (NDH-1, SDH, PSII, or PSI's cyclic flow) sends an electron (a pair) with its photons and its protons crossing up (queued in wall time, not timers). At each later complex the electron waits on its carrier (`gates` in particles.js) until that complex turns over, which pumps its protons, uses its O₂ or makes its NADPH. A turnover that no drawn electron meets within 0.6 s takes one from its donor carrier's pool (a short hop), and an electron that waits 3 s fades into its carrier's pool. ATP synthase's turnovers are thirds of a turn, forward or back, and NNT's are its exchange. FNR's turnovers run backward (in the dark) hand an NADPH's electrons to a ferredoxin, which joins the pool NDH-1 takes from; the ladder runs that electron from NADP⁺ through FNR to Fd. Map reactions that change a drawn pool in the slab's compartment are sampled at their one-way rates there (at most `MAX_EVENTS`, 3, a second per reaction, pool and direction), so an enzyme at equilibrium still turns molecules over: each event changes a molecule and runs a thread to the enzyme's connection (`Renderer.mapEvent`), which pulses. A complex that changes a pool (ATP synthase, FNR, NDH-1, NNT) draws the nearest molecule in to its docking site, holds it and lets it go changed, carried back out as far from the site as it came from (stroma.js), so a busy complex doesn't gather the pool around it: the real pools mix in microseconds, which the drawn drift is far too slow to show. Superoxide radicals appear at the model's production rate in the slab and each is cleared at the model's own rate per radical (dismutase's flux over the superoxide there), so inhibiting SOD keeps them on screen. Display noise never reaches the model.

### Cells, presets and specimens

cell.js holds the cell under the microscope. `Cell.use(preset, specimen)` builds a fresh engine for the preset's cell, applies its environment, warms it (40 model seconds, silently) and points the window and flux at it; `reset` does the same from the cell's starting state, keeping the environment and dials. A line's switch knocks its reactions out unless another switched-on line shares them (`LINE_REACTIONS`, mapbind.js); a line whose reactions the cell lacks leaves the key, which names it under the lines (`Absent:`), and its knockout checkbox is disabled. `Cell.pulse(target, sign, hold)` takes a segment (`seg:g5`), a complex (`mem:atpSyn`), a line (`line:krebs`) or an array of them, and pulses each reaction once however many segments draw it.

A hold (`hold` true) starts the paired baseline (compare.js; VISION.md, the verb). Two copies of the cell start from that moment, one held ×1.1 and one not, and run in a module worker until every flux in both is still (under 0.1% a second for 5 s, up to 900 model s). There each flux's response gives the held enzyme's flux control coefficient over it, C = ln(J_held/J_base)/ln 1.1 (Kacser & Burns 1973), for its own flux and the cell's system fluxes (`SYSTEM`: glucose uptake, carbon fixation, water splitting, O₂ use, ATP synthase, growth, export). Without a steady state none is reported. Changing the environment, a dial or a switch while it runs cancels it. Holds take 230 to 750 model seconds to settle, 30 to 80 ms of worker time.

Each preset shows its own membrane (`SPECIMEN_OF` in specimens.js): the cyanobacterium a thylakoid sac with phycobilisomes under PSII and galactolipid (ring-headed) lipids; the plant chloroplast a thylakoid whose photosystem II end sits in a granum (discs stacked above and below) and whose ATP synthase and PSI sit in the stroma lamella; the animal cell a mitochondrial crista, its tip bent by an ATP synthase dimer and its junction opening upward; the obligate anaerobe a bare plasma membrane under a peptidoglycan wall; the archaeon the halophile's purple membrane, with bacteriorhodopsin in a lattice, ether lipids (methyl-branched tails) and an S-layer. Custom shows the composite membrane with all fourteen complexes, on the composite cell. A specimen's `can` flags now only steer the drawing (what the first frame seeds, the key's marks).

### Timing

`main.js` runs one rAF loop: while playing, `Cell.engine.advance(dt * Win.speed, ENGINE_MS)` (the model clock is wall time at the clock's speed, as far as the chemistry gets in 8 ms), then `Win.sample(dt)` (evaluate the rates, write the picture, sample events), `updateFlux`, `Renderer.update` (rotor, O₂, particles, trains, all in wall time), Fig. 2's samples every 0.2 s of running time (trace.js; its clock stops while paused), the legend every 0.25 s, then the draw and the caption. The clock (`,` slower and `.` faster: `SPEEDS` in cell.js, 300×, 100×, 30×, 10× and 3× slower, real time, 3× to 1,000× faster) changes how much chemistry a second shows, not the chemistry. Steady states run at tens of thousands of model seconds a second and a switch's transient costs tens of milliseconds, so the chemistry stays on the main thread; where it falls short, `kept` (the share of the clock's time reached, smoothed over a second) puts the real speed in the caption.

Lumen opens on a living frame: at boot it applies the cyanobacterium preset (warming its cell), fills the stroma and seeds a representative instant (photons and electrons in transit). Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the model clock starts paused and the loop holds that frame still until the first interaction.

### Rendering

`Renderer` (renderer.js) owns the canvas and a shared `createCamera` whose clamp keeps the world in view; `minZoom` is 1, the fit. On wide screens `layout()` places the membrane (`Membrane.layout`, scaled so the block fits about 42% of the height) and the map below it (`MapView.build`), one world under one camera that fits the canvas exactly. At 760 px and below the page is a folio (`_layoutFolio`): panel a on the scene canvas at a fixed 3.4 px per nm, as wide as the cut needs, panned sideways and pinched (the page scrolls up and down, `touch-action: pan-y`, and the paper fades at an edge with more beyond it); panel b on its own canvas (`#map-canvas`), the map transposed upright at the screen's width with the key under it and the locator in its empty top right. Each canvas is as tall as it draws, and CSS orders the legend's sections between them. A ResizeObserver on the scene relays out.

The membrane's still parts (frame, lipids, protein bodies) are painted into an offscreen cache keyed by specimen, size, theme, damage and zoom (re-rendered once a zoom settles; lipid tails are drawn one by one only at 9 px per nm and up); rotors, particles and labels draw live. Draw order: cache, ATP synthase's rotor and head, stroma molecules and radicals, the map's lines, threads, the map's names (so a thread never crosses text), the zoom layer (detail.js, fading in from 2.2× to 2.6×), particles (clipped out of the colophon's corner), carrier sites, callouts, panel titles and scale bar, the locator.

The plate's furniture (plate.js) matches the siblings': complex names are callouts in rows above the cut with hairline leaders, so the lumen holds only protons; panel a is titled and carries a 10 nm scale bar (it is to scale; the map is not); a hairline and panel b's title sit between the registers. The key is one cartouche (mapdraw.js `drawKey`): the lines by carbon flux, each row its line's knockout switch (a box filled while its enzymes are on; clicking the row toggles it, `MapView.keyRows`), the cell's missing lines named once under them, and the marks, rows only for what the scene shows, with the pools' counts (molecules in the drawn slab, as at the stations). The locator (locator.js) in the map's top right is a drawing of the specimen's cell or organelle with the cut boxed; the legend's specimen row uses the same drawings as icons. Each membrane carries its signature lipids: MGDG, DGDG, SQDG and PG heads in thylakoids, cardiolipin gathered near the crista's tip, branched ether lipids in the archaeon; the anaerobe's wall is peptidoglycan threaded by lipoteichoic acid.

The map fits the cell's own network (`MapView._fit`): a sparse cell (the halophile's) gets the key and the locator in a column at its left when that draws the network larger; cells with most of the network keep the full grid, the key in its lower left and the locator top right. Names are placed so they don't collide (`drawMapText`): stations first (an interchange takes the next free side), loop names (the Krebs cycle's tries places inside its ring, then its short name; β-oxidation's shrinks to fit its square), enzymes' names (their side of the track, the other side, then nudged along it, inside the map), then gates (across the track from the name; at a crowded junction, after the name on its line). A pulsed or held enzyme carries its live activity multiplier (`×4.2`, `held ×1.1`) while it lasts, over its name on the side away from the track (`MapView.pulseOf`), and a complex after its callout (`Membrane.pulseOf`).

The map (maplayout.js, map.js, mapdraw.js) is laid out by hand on an octilinear grid. Stations are named for species (their counts are the window's); ticks mark single stations and capsules or rings the interchanges. Tracks are drawn lines, one ink each: glycolysis runs the corridor, the Calvin cycle runs back along it on its own offset track, the pentose phosphate pathway loops above and closes back along the corridor through PGI, the Krebs cycle is a ring line (a rounded square about `KC`, citrate at its corner and the other corner stations on the corners' curves, its names placed per station), β-oxidation a loop with an acetyl-CoA spur. mapbind.js binds each segment to its reactions with the carbon each carries along it per turnover (negative where it runs against the drawn way; in a cell with the Calvin cycle the corridor's reversible steps hand their backward flux to the Calvin track; PGI's backward flux closes the PPP's loop only up to G6PDH's forward rate, a part's `cap`, and the rest, storage or gluconeogenesis, stays on the corridor), so a segment with none of its reactions in the cell leaves the map. `FLIP` marks segments drawn against their reaction. Corridor enzymes that serve two tracks (`PAIR`) are labelled once, named for the way carbon runs; a segment with several reactions (the corridor's head: hexokinase, G6Pase, glycogen's phosphorylase and synthase) is named for the one carrying its flux. Line weight and train speed read each segment's carbon flux (flux.js: mM of carbon per second, matrix rates scaled to the cytosol's volume, smoothed for drawing; `RUNNING` is the idle threshold); trains are chevrons cut from the line, so a still frame shows direction. Every turn is a curve, but where a line that isn't offset turns at an interchange's ring where another line joins it (fatty acid, citrate): it turns inside the ring, so the lines meet at its centre. Lines switched off are dashed hairlines.

Each labelled enzyme carries its connections: the pools its main reaction makes (+) or spends (−) per turnover, from its stoichiometry (`MapView.applyCell`), as glyphs after its name, for the direction it is running; a loop's name carries its turn's textbook budget (`COF` in maplayout.js). They pulse when the window samples the enzyme changing a pool. On phones the upright map leaves them out. A gate (flux.js `segmentGate`) marks the effector holding a running enzyme back most, across the track from its name: a pool's glyph or a word (light, a metabolite), with ⊣ (inhibits) or → (needed), dashed while it only slows it; where the track is crowded it follows the name on its line, and where even that would cover a name or a station it is left out (the card still gives the regulation). A specimen with `zones` (the crista: the mitochondrial matrix) draws them as fare zones with a bilayer boundary.

Hit testing: membrane hit boxes (callouts included) first, then stroma molecules (tooltip only), the key's line rows (a click knocks the line out or back), loop names, segment labels, segments (within 9 screen px), and stations (tooltip only). A click pulses what it hits (a segment with its corridor twin, a complex, or a loop's whole line), right or middle click inhibits, and holding for 400 ms, with the mouse or a finger, sustains the hold until let go (losing the window lets go too); a touch tap pulses or inhibits by the mode (X), and a long press holds by it. Tooltips resolve through `_ETC_INFO`, `_SEG_INFO` and loop `info` keys into `ENZYMES`, and stations into `METABOLITES`, and end with the card's line: the main reaction's rate, ΔG and its three factors. A click also brings what it hits to the legend's card, and Fig. 2 marks it.

### Color, text, and theme

The canvas reads its palette from `_themePalette()` through `readPalette()` (complexes.js) on every theme change: the inks, the ground and text tones, and the accent, which is Lumen's one red and means protons. Each line and complex has an ink (`LINES` in maplayout.js; the shapes in complexes.js). colors.js only names the DOM's roles (`--pw-*`, `--co-*`). Canvas text is Newsreader through `_FONT.text`, at least 10 px, and the loop waits on `_loadTextFonts()` (or 1.5 s) before the first draw.

The theme has three states (theme.js): light, dark, and "follows sunlight" (dark while the light switch is off). It starts from the system preference, which `_colophon.mount` applies, and isn't stored. `visualLightMode` follows the page theme.

### The legend and UI

ui.js wires the colophon (play, theme, help), the legend, the `shortcuts` array (the source of truth for keys), the about panel, info tips, and the reference overlay (the legend's Reference row, one word a page: its only way in). The legend is a column of 280 to 320 px ruled off by a hairline (S toggles it), read top to bottom with no tabs:

- **The specimens**: the six locator drawings in a row, named by their `title`s. Choosing one puts its preset's cell under the microscope (`Cell.use`); its name follows.
- **The conditions** (legend.js): label–value rows whose values are the controls, built per cell (export instead of growth where the cell has `TPT`, the uncoupler where it has `UCP`). A value drags sideways (7 px a step), steps with the arrow keys (it is a slider or a switch to assistive technology), or opens a hairline dial under the rows on a click. The clock reads 300× slower to 1,000× faster left to right. Feeding (only what the cell can take up) and Reset follow. The knockouts are visually hidden checkboxes for the keyboard (they show when focused); the plate's key is their face.
- **The enzyme** (card.js): the one you last clicked on the plate, or chose in its select, which lists every complex and enzyme the cell has (each preset opens on its own, `FIRST` in ui.js). Its rate is a budget: capacity, then each factor the engine puts on it (a switch, light, a pulse, thermodynamics, saturation, regulation), each bar the running product on a log scale over three decades, what the factor took away hatched; over it, its ΔG (and `backward`); under it, while regulation holds it back, the two effectors doing most of it, as the map's gate glyphs with their factors. Pulse, Inhibit and Hold act on it; a hold reports its comparison's progress, then the control coefficients, which a toast also announces.
- **Fig. 2** (trace.js): the last two minutes of watching (its axis says how much of the cell's time they held: −4 s at 30× slower, −33 h at 1,000× faster, bent where the speed changed): Δp, the ATP share of the adenylates, the reduced share of the cell's main carrier (NADPH where it photosynthesizes, else NADH) and its headline flux (O₂ made or used, the halophile's ATP synthase, the anaerobe's glucose uptake), each value beside its name, with an arrow and a label for every change made (a drag's run moves one arrow). Its other view (V) is the live Z-scheme (ladder.js): every carrier at its midpoint potential, more reducing up, and each electron in flight as a bead on the carrier it has reached, with the membrane's yield (`yieldText`: ATP per NADPH by linear flow, the P/O ratios, H⁺ per ATP and the ring) from the cell's own stoichiometry; it is disabled (struck through) where the membrane has no chain. Under both, the readings: Δp's parts Δψ and ΔpH and the Δp ATP synthesis needs, ATP's sources (synthase, negative while it splits ATP, and substrates), the exchange with the medium and growth, the membrane's health and its superoxide and peroxide.

The legend's text follows the root AGENTS.md's "Show, don't tell": names, units and live values, no ledes, hints or commentary; readouts are label–value pairs (dashboard.js `pairs`, each kept on one line and broken only after a `·`). The Fig. 1 caption (main.js) is the plate's live values in a line: Δp, ATP synthase's drawn turns a second (negative backward), when thinned, how many turnovers one drawn stands for, and, while the chemistry can't keep up, the clock's real speed. The specimen is panel a's title, the conditions are the legend's and each line's flux is the key's, so the caption doesn't repeat them.

On phones the legend's sections sit in the page between the plate's canvases (see Rendering), and "taps" under the plate switches taps between pulse and inhibit (X).

## Conventions

- The engine is authoritative and headless: nothing under src/engine/ touches the page, and nothing outside it writes the engine's state except through cell.js (switches, environment, dials, pulses, feeding). The window writes `store`; nothing reads `store` back into the model.
- A new reaction goes in network.js balanced in elements and charge (the tests check it against BiGG's formulas; add a species' BiGG id to tools/bigg.mjs and regenerate formulas.js), into the cells that have it (cells.js), with its ΔG'° (thermo.js: eQuilibrator's, or from potentials) and its kinetics (rates.js). Run the tests: a new conservation law nobody specified, or a cycle with a ΔG'° of its own, is a finding.
- Parameters are illustrative unless a comment cites a source; a measured value names it.
- colors.js is a deferred classic script ordered before the `main.js` module. Its `_freezeTokens()` call freezes `_PALETTE`, so new palette keys go above it.
- The about-panel `lastUpdated` is in `src/ui.js`. The parent build rewrites it, along with index.html's title, descriptions, and `dateModified`, from about.md's frontmatter.
- Every effect in the scene reads a quantity in the model (the root AGENTS.md, "Inside the scene"). A new one names its quantity in a comment where it is drawn.
- A new membrane complex touches specimens.js (`WIDTH`, the specimens' `order` and `names`), complexes.js (its part list and painter), membrane.js (`drawStatic`, its anchor height in `layout`, its hit box, `TOP_NM` for its callout, its stromal parts in `_stroma`, a docking site if it changes a pool), detail.js (its cofactor sites and subunits), ladder.js (its carriers), renderer.js (`_ETC_INFO`), info.js (`ENZYMES`), mapbind.js (`COMPLEX`), rates.js (`KCAT`), window.js if its turnovers draw something new, and the engine's network for its chemistry.
- A new map segment touches maplayout.js (its track's `segs`, a station if new), mapbind.js (`segments`, `STATION` if new), map.js (`LABEL_AT`, phone overrides, `FLIP`), and renderer.js (`_SEG_INFO`).

## Open work

`docs/VISION.md` (not staged) holds the three ideation rounds (2026-10-24, 10-25 and 10-27) and a9's decisions on them: where Lumen could go, the engine's design, round 3's audit of what was built, and the milestones. Milestones 0, 0.5 and 1 are built (the engine's phases A and B landed on 2026-10-25), and the third visual pass (2026-10-26; `docs/third-pass/` has its proposal, the motion probe and its handoff) made the margin the figure's legend and gave Fig. 2 its first trace. Round 3 (2026-10-27; `docs/round-3/` has its proposal page, which runs the engine live) audited the build. a9 declined its record-centred bench and returned the cells to archetypes (decisions 13–14): Lumen is a live, causal, observational sandbox of a form of life, with a control run only beside a poison. The roadmap has three milestones: day and night, with a clock that runs past life (2; the clock and the night are built, the day is next); the flagship made whole, with the CCM, carbon and nitrogen, and transport (3); and growth (4). Behaviour that adds to the textbook reading without complicating it enters freely; identity detail that only complicates it stays out (decision 16). Poisons are a side track.

Known approximations, to revisit as the engine matures (VISION.md, "What round 3 found", has the numbers):

- Most parameters are illustrative (rates.js), and most pool sizes too (cells.js); SABIO-RK's constants and each organism's measured pools should replace them. The biomass row is E. coli's core composition in every cell.
- The cyanobacterium has no carbon-concentrating mechanism, so at full sun its CO₂ is drawn down to 2 µM and its 3-PGA to about 0.1 mM (milestone 3).
- Growth is starved at α-KG (the cyanobacterium makes 2% of its dial) and its products aren't accounted anywhere; the map has no spurs "to the cell" (milestones 3 and 4). So by day nearly all fixed carbon goes to the store: glycogen or starch climbs by about 3 M (glucosyl units) over a 12-hour day, where a real cell's would rise a few hundred mM.
- K⁺ is held on both sides, so the counter-ion conductances are standing leaks; the thylakoid's are small so that a night's few protons aren't lost to them. The chloroplast exports from its starch as triose, standing in for the maltose and glucose of a real leaf (MEX1, pGlcT), and faster than a leaf, which paces its starch to last until dawn by its clock; its cytosol is an open reservoir (triose held low), so with no starch the translocator brings a little back. The night's budget (the cyanobacterium's maintenance demand, its mid-morning store, the thylakoid's leak and counter-ion conductances) is illustrative and sets how long a night the cell lasts; that it lasts doesn't confirm those values. The composite cell in anoxic dark still runs out of ATP. IF1 is still a Δp gate, where AtpΘ and CF₁ are brakes.
- Identities simplify for legibility, by design (decision 14): the cyanobacterium's Krebs cycle is the textbook ring, with α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase and succinyl-CoA synthetase, which most cyanobacteria lack (Zhang & Bryant 2011), and its lactate is L, not D.
- Fatty-acid synthesis runs on acyl-CoA, not ACP, lumped per round; the animal cell makes no fatty acid (it would need the citrate shuttle out of the matrix). The matrix's carriers are lumped (milestone 3 draws their anatomy).
- The halophile has no amino-acid metabolism, so without light it has nothing to run on.
- The lumen's drawn protons stand for 2 mV each, not for counts; PSII's centre states aren't modeled.
- The hold's steady state checks fluxes only, and Fig. 2's O₂ is PSII's gross production, not net exchange.
- The pulse's size and fade (×5 at its peak, 1.5 model seconds) and the hold's 10% are first guesses, to tune by eye.
